The Architecture · B3

How opportunities reach you.

From SAM.gov’s public API to your inbox or webhook, here is the path each opportunity takes.

Each morning at 09:00 Eastern, OpenSAM queries SAM.gov’s Public API for active opportunities matching your saved searches — NAICS codes, set-aside types, agencies, deadline windows. The response is a JSON array; we map it into typed SamOpportunity objects via @opensam/sam-gov-types.

For each opportunity, the @opensam/scoring engine runs: NAICS match (+25), set-aside alignment (+10 to +15), capability keyword matches (+5 each, capped at +20), deadline proximity penalty (−15 or −35). Base 50. Clamp 0–100. The score is deterministic — same input, same output, every time. No AI credits, no opaque weights.

Scored opportunities are sorted descending. High fit (≥70) fires a webhook to your Slack or email immediately. Medium fit (40–69) lands in the daily digest at 09:00 ET. Low fit (<40) is dropped — not because the opportunity is bad, but because it is not for you.

The hosted API at api.opensam.us exposes the same SDK methods over HTTPS. Pro and Enterprise customers hit the same endpoints; the difference is rate limit, region, and SLA. Free customers run the SDK locally against api.sam.gov directly — same code, different operator.

The Editorial · B4

Why we charge for free software.

OpenSAM is open-source under the MIT License. The source code lives at github.com/eddyflores100-lang/OpenSAM. You can fork it, modify it, sell services on top of it, and never pay us a cent. That is the license, and we will not change it.

So what, exactly, are you paying for when you subscribe to Pro or Enterprise? Not the code. The infrastructure, the people, and the legal posture around the code.

The Free plan is not a trial.

If you are an individual contractor, a small business owner, or a developer building an AI agent, the Free plan is the whole product. There is no feature lock, no rate-limit bait-and-switch, no upsell nag. The scoring engine, the SDK, the type definitions — all of it, MIT-licensed, forever. If you outgrow the api.data.gov daily limit of 1,000 calls, you can bring your own key, or you can upgrade. Both are honest answers.

Pro is for teams that do not want to operate infrastructure.

A capture team of five does not want to maintain a Supabase project, an Edge Function pipeline, and a Slack webhook relay. Pro gives them a hosted endpoint, a shared workspace, daily digest, webhook alerts, and someone to call when the SDK throws a SamRateLimitError on a Friday afternoon. The price is $49 per seat per month, billed annually or monthly. The first 14 days are free; no credit card required.

Enterprise is for organizations that cannot use someone else’s cloud.

If you are a prime contractor, a federally-funded research and development center, or an agency-adjacent nonprofit, you probably cannot ship SAM.gov opportunity data through a third-party SaaS. You need the software running in your own VPC, behind your own SSO, with your own audit log. Enterprise is that: the same OpenSAM code, deployed on your infrastructure, with a dedicated solutions engineer, a 99.9% uptime SLA with service credits, quarterly penetration test reports, and a FedRAMP Moderate readiness package (currently in progress, target Q2 2027).

What we will never do.

We will never close the source. We will never add telemetry to the open-source packages. We will never sell your SAM.gov opportunity history. We will never email you marketing unless you explicitly ask. We will never require a credit card for the Free plan. We will never deprecate the public REST endpoints without 12 months’ notice. These commitments are written into the Terms of Service as enforceable contract terms, not marketing copy.

Letters to the Editor · B5

Pricing questions, answered.

Is the Free plan really free forever, or is it a trial? Free forever. The MIT License is irrevocable. The code at github.com/eddyflores100-lang/OpenSAM is the product. There is no trial clock.

What happens if I exceed 1,000 API calls per day on Free? The api.data.gov key is rate-limited by the U.S. government, not by us. If you exceed 1,000 calls, you wait until the next day, you bring your own api.data.gov key (free, separate limit), or you upgrade to Pro. We never cut you off; the limit is upstream.

Do Pro plans auto-renew? Can I cancel anytime? Monthly plans renew monthly until you cancel. Annual plans renew annually. Cancel anytime from the dashboard or by emailing contacto@alicelabs.site. Refunds are governed by our Refund Policy.

Is there a non-profit or academic discount? Yes. Registered 501(c)(3) nonprofits, accredited academic institutions, and FFRDCs get 50% off Pro. Email contacto@alicelabs.site with proof and we will issue a coupon.

Do you sign Data Processing Agreements (DPAs)? Yes. Enterprise customers get a signed DPA as part of the master agreement. The standard DPA is at opensam.us/dpa.

Can I get a custom SLA? Enterprise customers can negotiate custom SLAs, including 99.99% uptime, dedicated capacity, and named-engineer response times. Custom terms are negotiated per contract.

What payment methods do you accept? Pro plans are billed via Stripe (Visa, Mastercard, Amex, ACH for annual). Enterprise plans can pay by invoice, wire, or purchase order. We do not accept cryptocurrency.

Where is my data stored? Pro plan data is stored in the United States (AWS us-east-1). Enterprise customers choose their region. We do not store SAM.gov opportunity data longer than 30 days for Free users or 90 days for Pro users; Enterprise retention is configurable. See our Privacy Policy.

Are you FedRAMP authorized? Not yet. We are pursuing FedRAMP Moderate readiness with a target authorization of Q2 2027. Enterprise customers can join the FedRAMP customer advisory group; email contacto@alicelabs.site with subject “FedRAMP advisory.”

Can I get a refund if I’m not satisfied? Yes — see the Refund Policy. Short version: 30-day no-questions-asked on monthly Pro; pro-rated on annual Pro; service-credit-only on Enterprise per the SLA.
